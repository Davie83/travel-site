On a first trip to Korea, **the things that trip you up are not the food or the sights.** They are entry clearance, the transit card, changing money, finding your way, and what to do when nobody speaks your language.

Know what is on this page and your first day gets much easier.

> **Prices and rules change.** This page was verified in September 2026. Where an amount is written, check it once more on the ground.

## 1. Entry requirements — check K-ETA before you fly {#visa}

**Unlike everything else on this page, this is the one thing you finish before departure, not after you arrive.**

**What K-ETA is** — an online pre-travel authorization that citizens of visa-waiver countries must get before flying to Korea. It is a separate process from a visa.

- **Currently waived through 31 December 2026** — citizens of 22 countries, including the US, Canada, Japan, Australia and Germany, can enter with just a passport, no K-ETA needed. Check whether your country is on the list at the [official K-ETA site](https://www.k-eta.go.kr) by selecting your nationality
- Even if you are exempt, **applying anyway skips the paper arrival card at immigration** — worth doing if you have the time
- If your country is not exempt, **apply on the official site at least a few days before departure** — approval can take time

**Watch out for lookalike sites.** Searching "K-ETA" often surfaces paid agency ads before the official site, and those agencies charge several times the real fee. Before you apply, check the address is exactly **k-eta.go.kr**.

> The waiver has been extended more than once, but it remains a **temporary measure**. Check the current status again right before you fly.

## 2. Transit card — one T-money card covers almost everything {#transport}

A single **T-money** card gets you on buses and subways across most of the country. It also works in taxis and convenience stores.

**Where to buy one**

- **Convenience stores** — GS25, CU, 7-Eleven, Emart24. Ask at the counter for a "T-money card"
- **Subway station machines** — every Seoul Metro station has them. The same machine sells and recharges
- **The airport** — buy at a convenience store in the arrivals area and load it immediately for the ride into the city

**What it costs**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Basic card | **2,500 ~ 4,000 KRW** |
| Character editions (Kakao Friends, BT21, Sanrio) | 5,000 ~ 10,000 KRW |
| Credit loaded on top | As much as you like (separate from the card price) |

**Important — the price of the card itself is not refundable.** You can get unused credit back, but the 2,500~4,000 KRW you paid for the card is gone. Treat it as a souvenir.

**Recharging** — as of 2026 most subway recharge machines accept foreign-issued **Visa, Mastercard, JCB, UnionPay and AmEx**. It used to be cash only; that is no longer the case.

**There is a tourist-only card too** — the old "Korea Tour Card" has been discontinued and replaced by the **T-Money Travel Card** (4,000 KRW, with partner discounts). The 6,000 KRW **Premium** version adds WOWPASS-style features: IC payment where foreign cards get declined, and won withdrawal. Buy it at a convenience store or the bus ticket counter at Incheon Airport.

**From Incheon Airport into the city** — I have taken the airport railway (AREX) to Seoul Station and to Hongik University Station (Hongdae), and a limousine bus to Ewha Womans University Station.

- **Going into Seoul — take the train.** Seoul Station and Hongdae are both reached directly on the airport railway, and it is the easy choice
- **Heading straight on to another region — take the limousine bus for that area.** It is far better than going into Seoul first and changing from there
- The limousine ride to Ewha Womans University Station took a little over an hour, as I remember it. I did not note the exact time, so check the timetable for your stop
- **If you would rather book ahead** — you can buy an [airport railway (AREX) ticket into central Seoul](https://affiliate.klook.com/redirect?aid=139889&aff_adid=1513149&k_site=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.klook.com%2Fko%2Factivity%2F1163-airport-to-seoul-city-center-arex-train-incheon) or an [Incheon Airport limousine bus ticket](https://affiliate.klook.com/redirect?aid=139889&aff_adid=1513151&k_site=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.klook.com%2Fko%2Factivity%2F121308-incheon-airport-limousine-bus-ticket) on Klook. *(Affiliate links: I may earn a commission if you book, at no extra cost to you.)*

## 3. Changing money — do not do it all at the airport {#money}

Rates vary a lot by location. Listed **best first**.

| Where | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Myeongdong / Namdaemun money changers** | **Best** | Cash only. Limited opening hours |
| **WOWPASS** | Bank level | 24-hour kiosks. **Doubles as a transit card** |
| Banks | Bank level | Weekday daytime only. Passport required |
| Airport exchange counters | **Worst** | Only when you must |

**At the airport, change only what you need to reach the city.** Do the rest downtown.

**Why WOWPASS is worth knowing about**

For a foreign visitor this is usually the easiest option. **Currency exchange, transit card and payments on one card.**

- **Feed foreign banknotes into the kiosk** and it converts them to won, loaded onto your card
- Accepts **16 currencies** (USD, EUR, JPY, GBP, AUD, CAD, CNY and more)
- **Tap the card** for subways, buses and taxis
- Available 24 hours, so bank opening times stop mattering

**Where do you get the card? The machine issues it on the spot**

There is no counter to visit and no application form. **The kiosk prints and dispenses the card there and then.**

- Around **444 machines** nationwide (official figure) — hotels, convenience stores, subway stations, tourist spots
- **Airports have them** — Incheon T1 and T2, Gimpo, Jeju, Gimhae (Busan), Daegu. Also Incheon Port and Busan Port
- In Seoul: **Myeongdong, Hongik University Station, Dongdaemun (DDP), Seoul Station**. In Busan: **Haeundae, Seomyeon**
- **The card fee is shown on the machine screen.** Check the amount before you confirm (changes often — please verify)
- To settle it in advance, **reserve online on the official site** and collect the card after you land
- Once you have it, **register the card in the WOWPASS app** — balance, freezing a lost card, and topping up the transit balance are all done there
- **Korean nationals cannot be issued a card.** The service is for foreign visitors only

**Three things to know before you go — these matter**

1. **The machines at Incheon Airport cannot exchange foreign cash.** The T1 and T2 machines (inside CU convenience stores) only **issue cards and top up in Korean won**. To turn dollars or yen into won, use a machine in the city. **Gimpo Airport offers the full service**; Jeju Airport cannot exchange cash
2. **There are two separate balances.** The payment balance (you **insert** the card) and the T-money transit balance (you **tap** the card) are **not the same pot**. The transit balance must be topped up separately, at a subway station or in the app. Not knowing this leaves you stuck at the gate
3. **It does not work for online payments or delivery apps.** Offline stores only

Top-up limits are **1,000,000 KRW at once, 2,000,000 per day, 10,000,000 per week**. Topping up with a foreign card in the app is capped at 200,000 per transaction and 1,000,000 per month.

**If the rate is all you care about**, the money changers in Myeongdong or Namdaemun are still the best. You just need cash in hand, and they close.

## 4. Paying — Korea runs on cards {#pay}

**You do not need to carry much cash.** Restaurants, cafes, convenience stores and taxis nearly all take cards.

A few exceptions:

- **Foreign-issued cards are occasionally declined**, especially on older terminals in small shops. Keep cash or a T-money / WOWPASS card as backup
- **Traditional markets and street stalls** are sometimes cash only
- Apple Pay acceptance is still limited

Carrying **50,000~100,000 KRW in cash** and paying for everything else by card works well.

## 5. Getting around — do not rely on Google Maps alone {#maps}

This one matters especially in Korea.

**Google Maps does not provide driving directions in Korea.** It is blocked by legal and mapping-data restrictions. Subway and bus directions do work.

So split it up like this:

| For | Use |
|---|---|
| Subway and bus | Google Maps is fine |
| **Driving, detailed walking routes** | **Naver Map** or **KakaoMap** (both have English) |
| Calling a taxi | **Kakao T** |
| Finding places and opening hours | Google Maps, Naver Map |

**Naver Map and KakaoMap both support English.** Download one before you arrive.

**To plan a route in advance**, use the [Saved places](/en/saved) feature on this site. Add the places you want in order and it draws the route on a map, with each leg handing off to Google Maps directions.

## 6. When nobody speaks your language — 1330 {#help}

**This is the most valuable line on this page.** Save one number.

# 1330

A **free, 24-hour** travel helpline run by the Korea Tourism Organization.

- **Eight languages** — Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay
- Directions, transport, restaurants, accommodation bookings, complaints
- **They interpret.** If you are stuck in a shop, call 1330 and hand the phone to the staff — the operator will interpret for both of you

**How to dial**

- Landline in Korea — `1330`
- Mobile in Korea — `area code + 1330` (Seoul `02-1330`, Busan `051-1330`, Jeju `064-1330`)
- From abroad — `82 + area code + 1330`

**For emergencies, dial 119** — fire, ambulance and rescue all go to 119. Police is 112.

## 7. There is no tipping {#tipping}

**Korea has no tipping culture.** Not in restaurants, cafes, taxis or hotels.

- The amount on the bill is **the whole amount**. Service is already included
- Leave a tip on the table and **staff will often chase you down to return it**
- Where a service charge does apply — some luxury hotels, some fine dining — it is **stated on the receipt**

Not tipping is not rude. It is simply how it works.

## 8. Restaurants — what to expect {#dining}

Korean restaurants differ from what you may be used to.

- **Side dishes (banchan) are free and refillable.** Refills are usually free too; rice and soup depend on the restaurant
- **Set meals often require one order per person.** Even when the portions look huge, two people sharing one order may not be allowed
- **You usually pay at the counter on the way out**, not at the table
- **Water is often self-service.** Help yourself from the dispenser near the entrance
- **Some restaurants have floor seating and you remove your shoes.** Wearing socks makes this easier
- **If there is a call button on the table, press it** to get a server. Raising your hand is also fine
- Wet wipes or extra plates are **occasionally charged**

**Break times and booking — what I keep running into in these guides**

- **Many restaurants close between lunch and dinner.** Tsuruhashi Fugetsu in Myeongdong has a break from 14:30 to 17:00 and Jinsang Hoetjip from 14:30 to 16:30 (as of Aug–Oct 2026). There is also a separate last-order time, so check before you go
- **Many places close at weekends.** In office neighbourhoods like Yeouido, some shops shut on Saturday and Sunday. Gwonsusan is closed on weekends, and so is the main Jinjin Mandu-guk branch near Yeouido Station (as of Aug 2026)
- **Some places need a booking.** Gwonsusan uses CatchTable, Kim & Kim Snow Crab uses Naver booking, and Seosan Kkotge works by reservation only
- **Some dishes are sold only at weekday lunch.** The sashimi set at Maknae Hoejip runs only at weekday lunch, at 14,000 KRW per person (as of Aug 2026)
- **Restaurants inside markets tend to close early.** Buwon Myeonok closes at 8:00 PM and is easy to miss on the second floor (as of Aug 2026)
- **Five-day markets open only on set days.** The Jeju Folk Five-Day Market runs on days ending in 2 or 7, and Ganghwa Pungmul Market also has a market on the 2nd and 7th (as of Oct 2026)
- **Places with queues start queueing before opening.** At Yokosuka Tsunami people line up before it opens, and at Dorimhang you need to go early in the evening to get a seat
- **To order, say the dish name plus "juseyo".** Each guide here gives a one-line phrase to order with, and you can say it as written, for example "Baguette juseyo"

## 9. Other things worth knowing {#daily}

- **Toilets are free and everywhere** — subway stations, department stores, parks. In subway stations they are often inside the ticket gates
- **Use convenience stores.** Open 24 hours, with meals, drinks, basic medicine, umbrellas and T-money recharging

- **Tap water is drinkable**, though most people use filtered water or bottled
- **Power is 220V with the round two-pin European plug.** US and Japanese plugs need an adapter

## Your first day, in order {#firstday}

1. At the airport, **change only enough to reach the city**
2. Buy and load a **T-money card** at the airport, or have a machine issue a **WOWPASS card** (at Incheon you can only get the card — **exchange cash in the city**)
3. Install **Naver Map or KakaoMap**
4. Save **1330**
5. Change the rest downtown (Myeongdong / Namdaemun, or a WOWPASS kiosk)

Those five things will get you through day one without getting stuck. (K-ETA is not on this list because it is a before-you-fly step, not a day-one one — see item 1.)
