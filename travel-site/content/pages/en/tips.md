On a first trip to Korea, **the things that trip you up are not the food or the sights.** They are the transit card, changing money, finding your way, and what to do when nobody speaks your language.

Know what is on this page and your first day gets much easier.

> **Prices and rules change.** This page was verified in August 2026. Where an amount is written, check it once more on the ground.

## 1. Transit card — one T-money card covers almost everything {#transport}

A single **T-money** card gets you on buses and subways across most of the country. It also works in taxis and convenience stores.

**Where to buy one**

- **Convenience stores** — GS25, CU, 7-Eleven, Emart24. Ask at the counter for a "T-money card"
- **Subway station machines** — every Seoul Metro station has them. The same machine sells and recharges
- **The airport** — buy at a convenience store in the arrivals area and load it immediately for the ride into the city

**What it costs**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Basic card | **2,500 ~ 4,000 KRW** |
| Character editions (Kakao Friends, BT21, Sanrio) | 5,000 ~ 10,000 KRW |
| Credit loaded on top | As much as you like (separate from the card price) |

**Important — the price of the card itself is not refundable.** You can get unused credit back, but the 2,500~4,000 KRW you paid for the card is gone. Treat it as a souvenir.

**Recharging** — as of 2026 most subway recharge machines accept foreign-issued **Visa, Mastercard, JCB, UnionPay and AmEx**. It used to be cash only; that is no longer the case.

## 2. Changing money — do not do it all at the airport {#money}

Rates vary a lot by location. Listed **best first**.

| Where | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Myeongdong / Namdaemun money changers** | **Best** | Cash only. Limited opening hours |
| **WOWPASS** | Bank level | 24-hour kiosks. **Doubles as a transit card** |
| Banks | Bank level | Weekday daytime only. Passport required |
| Airport exchange counters | **Worst** | Only when you must |

**At the airport, change only what you need to reach the city.** Do the rest downtown.

**Why WOWPASS is worth knowing about**

For a foreign visitor this is usually the easiest option. **Currency exchange, transit card and payments on one card.**

- **Feed foreign banknotes into the kiosk** and it converts them to won, loaded onto your card
- Accepts **16 currencies** (USD, EUR, JPY, GBP, AUD, CAD, CNY and more)
- **T-money is built in**, so the same card taps you onto the subway and buses
- **Over 320 kiosk locations** nationwide — major subway stations, hotels, shopping malls
- Available 24 hours, so bank opening times stop mattering

**If the rate is all you care about**, the money changers in Myeongdong or Namdaemun are still the best. You just need cash in hand, and they close.

## 3. Paying — Korea runs on cards {#pay}

**You do not need to carry much cash.** Restaurants, cafes, convenience stores and taxis nearly all take cards.

A few exceptions:

- **Foreign-issued cards are occasionally declined**, especially on older terminals in small shops. Keep cash or a T-money / WOWPASS card as backup
- **Traditional markets and street stalls** are sometimes cash only
- Apple Pay acceptance is still limited

Carrying **50,000~100,000 KRW in cash** and paying for everything else by card works well.

## 4. Getting around — do not rely on Google Maps alone {#maps}

This one matters especially in Korea.

**Google Maps does not provide driving directions in Korea.** It is blocked by legal and mapping-data restrictions. Subway and bus directions do work.

So split it up like this:

| For | Use |
|---|---|
| Subway and bus | Google Maps is fine |
| **Driving, detailed walking routes** | **Naver Map** or **KakaoMap** (both have English) |
| Calling a taxi | **Kakao T** |
| Finding places and opening hours | Google Maps, Naver Map |

**Naver Map and KakaoMap both support English.** Download one before you arrive.

**To plan a route in advance**, use the [Saved places](saved) feature on this site. Add the places you want in order and it draws the route on a map, with each leg handing off to Google Maps directions.

## 5. When nobody speaks your language — 1330 {#help}

**This is the most valuable line on this page.** Save one number.

# 1330

A **free, 24-hour** travel helpline run by the Korea Tourism Organization.

- **Eight languages** — Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay
- Directions, transport, restaurants, accommodation bookings, complaints
- **They interpret.** If you are stuck in a shop, call 1330 and hand the phone to the staff — the operator will interpret for both of you

**How to dial**

- Landline in Korea — `1330`
- Mobile in Korea — `area code + 1330` (Seoul `02-1330`, Busan `051-1330`, Jeju `064-1330`)
- From abroad — `82 + area code + 1330`

**For emergencies, dial 119** — fire, ambulance and rescue all go to 119. Police is 112.

## 6. There is no tipping {#tipping}

**Korea has no tipping culture.** Not in restaurants, cafes, taxis or hotels.

- The amount on the bill is **the whole amount**. Service is already included
- Leave a tip on the table and **staff will often chase you down to return it**
- Where a service charge does apply — some luxury hotels, some fine dining — it is **stated on the receipt**

Not tipping is not rude. It is simply how it works.

## 7. Restaurants — what to expect {#dining}

Korean restaurants differ from what you may be used to.

- **Side dishes (banchan) are free and refillable.** Refills are usually free too; rice and soup depend on the restaurant
- **Set meals often require one order per person.** Even when the portions look huge, two people sharing one order may not be allowed
- **You usually pay at the counter on the way out**, not at the table
- **Water is often self-service.** Help yourself from the dispenser near the entrance
- **Some restaurants have floor seating and you remove your shoes.** Wearing socks makes this easier
- **If there is a call button on the table, press it** to get a server. Raising your hand is also fine
- Wet wipes or extra plates are **occasionally charged**

## 8. Other things worth knowing {#daily}

- **Toilets are free and everywhere** — subway stations, department stores, parks. In subway stations they are often inside the ticket gates
- **Use convenience stores.** Open 24 hours, with meals, drinks, basic medicine, umbrellas and T-money recharging
- **Public WiFi** is common on subways, buses and in cafes. A SIM or eSIM is still far more convenient
- **Tap water is drinkable**, though most people use filtered water or bottled
- **Power is 220V with the round two-pin European plug.** US and Japanese plugs need an adapter
- **Tax refund** — spend above a threshold and you can reclaim the tax. Show your passport at the shop for the paperwork or an immediate refund. Thresholds and limits change often, so confirm in store

## Your first day, in order {#firstday}

1. At the airport, **change only enough to reach the city**
2. Buy and load a **T-money card** at an airport convenience store (or get WOWPASS downtown)
3. Install **Naver Map or KakaoMap**
4. Save **1330**
5. Change the rest downtown (Myeongdong / Namdaemun, or a WOWPASS kiosk)

Those five things will get you through day one without getting stuck.
