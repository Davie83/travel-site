Driving past Sapgyocheon for work, what made me pull over wasn't the bread — it was a hanok that caught my eye.

## Lured in by the building, not the bread

Right at the entrance there's a big display of what looks like a **master-baker licence**, and inside, a large hanok timber frame carries clean modern finishes on top of it. The wooden ceiling battens, the light coming through the lattice windows — it's a good place to sit with a coffee. Looking it up, it turned out to be a bakery cafe fairly well known around here, and my first impression wasn't the bread; it was the **space**.

![Inside Inju Cafe hanok branch — a big communal table under the hanok frame and a wooden lattice ceiling](/assets/img/asan-inju-cafe-interior.jpg)

## The "master baker" pitch, and the bread itself

There's a fair amount of signage inside saying "a master baker bakes here." So I went in with expectations raised — but honestly, it was **more ordinary than I expected.** The range is actually **on the small side** for a bakery cafe, and the few I picked up and ate on the road weren't a "I need to come back for this" taste. Not that they're badly made; just not up to what the space had promised.

## Still enough reason to linger

Set the bread aside and this is **a good place to put a tired head down for a while**. The big hanok interior isn't crowded, and with a coffee and the view out the window, time slows down. As a mid-errand breather, it was enough.

## What foreign visitors should know

- It's a **bakery cafe**. You pick bread from the display and pay at the counter; coffee is ordered separately at the counter. No table service.
- A whole hanok converted into one large room — good for photos. Table-and-chair seating, not floor seating.
- It's far from Seoul. You need a **rental car** to reach it (near the Sapgyocheon sea wall).
- **"Myeongjang" (名匠, "master artisan")** is a title the government gives to top practitioners in a given craft. This place puts its confectionery master front and centre in its marketing.
- ✅ Good if you're going for the **hanok space and mood**, or need somewhere to stop while **driving through** Sapgyocheon or Asan.
- ⚠️ Less good if you put the bread itself first, or if you're relying on public transport.

Approach it as a restaurant and it disappoints; approach it as a **hanok cafe** and it's a decent stop.

## Before you go

- **Getting there** — near the Sapgyocheon sea wall, Inju-myeon, Asan, Chungnam. Check the name on the map before you set off.
- **Transport** — a rental car is assumed. There's a large car park in front.
- **Ordering** — pick bread at the display and pay, coffee separately at the counter. Buying a few to eat on the road works fine.
- **Hours · prices · closing day** — daily 10:00–22:00 (last order 21:00), Americano around ₩6,500, no fixed closing day found. This can still change, so check the map or call before you go.
- **Elsewhere in Chungcheong** — up in Jecheon, Chungbuk, I also stopped by [Cheongpung Resort](/en/posts/jecheon-cheongpung-resort), which looks out over Cheongpung Lake.
