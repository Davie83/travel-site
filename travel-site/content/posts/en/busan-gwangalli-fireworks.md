I hadn't planned it. I went to Busan for a schedule, and then learned that the day after it was the Busan Fireworks Festival, held at Gwangalli Beach once a year, so I decided to stay one more day.

## 🎆 One more day for the fireworks

During my time in Busan I found out that the Busan Fireworks Festival would be held at Gwangalli Beach the very next day. It was the 21st edition this year, on Saturday, 7 November 2026. So I stayed an extra day.

## 🏙️ Gwangalli after a few years, and how it has changed

I can't remember how many years it has been since I was last at Gwangalli Beach. In the meantime many apartments and large buildings have gone up around it, and the scenery has changed on either side of the raw-fish center building near the middle.

In my twenties I loved Busan, Korea's second city, and I made time to come at least once a year. From my thirties I couldn't come every year, using being busy as an excuse, but I kept trying to and did. The timing never worked out, though, so I had never seen the fireworks festival.

## ☕ The day before: coffee and fine sand

This is Gwangalli Beach the day before the festival. Just sitting quietly with a coffee and looking at the fine sand is wonderfully relaxing.

![Gwangalli Beach the day before the festival — a stone path curves across the broad sandy beach while people walk toward the sea](/assets/img/busan-gwangalli-fireworks-beach.jpg)

In the photo, a grey stone path curves gently across the broad sandy beach, and people walk along the water. High-rise buildings line the shoreline on the right, and tall buildings rise in the distance on the left.

## ⚓ The east waterfront park is a harbour

This time it's the direction of the eastern waterfront park. Unlike the relaxed beach, this side works as a harbour, a route for fishing boats. Marine City can be seen far off.

![A harbour near the east waterfront park — fishing boats moored thickly beside calm water, with a red lighthouse, a bridge and the high-rises of Marine City beyond](/assets/img/busan-gwangalli-fireworks-harbor.jpg)

In the photo, fishing boats of all sizes are moored tightly along both sides of a calm harbour. Birds that look like gulls float on the water, and in the distance a red lighthouse, what looks like Gwangan Bridge and the high-rises of Marine City line up.

## 🌅 Festival morning: paid seats already set up

This is the sun rising on the morning of the festival. By then the beach already had the festival's paid seats set up.

![Gwangalli Beach on festival morning — the sun rising beyond Gwangan Bridge, with rows of black plastic chairs across the sand](/assets/img/busan-gwangalli-fireworks-sunrise.jpg)

In the photo, the sun rises close to the horizon and orange light spreads toward Gwangan Bridge, while rows of black plastic chairs fill the sand. A few people walk along the shore in the distance.

## 🎇 The hour passed in a flash

After a long wait, evening came, and the beach, the nearby roads and even the buildings were all full of people.

![The 21st Busan Fireworks Festival — golden fireworks pouring down the night sky, the pink-lit Gwangan Bridge, and spectators holding up phones](/assets/img/busan-gwangalli-fireworks.jpg)

Compared with the Hangang Fireworks Festival it felt slightly smaller in scale, but watching the fireworks, everyone wore a happy look. People are surely animals who love playing with fire. And so the festival's one hour of fireworks passed in an instant and was over. It was a pity, but I'd like to see it again next year if the timing works.

In the photo, golden fireworks pour down like willow branches high in the night sky, with red and purple bursts between them. Below, a bridge lit in pink is visible, and spectators hold their phones up.

## 🌐 For visitors

- Busan is Korea's second city, and Gwangalli Beach is one of its beaches
- The Busan Fireworks Festival happens only once a year and the schedule changes every year, so check the official notice before you go
- If you arrive the day before, the beach is much more relaxed. It was lovely to walk the sand with a cup of coffee
- Unlike the beach, the eastern waterfront park side has the feel of a harbour for fishing boats

If you have a schedule in Busan that happens to overlap with the festival, it is worth staying one more day. Since it is a once-a-year event, waiting for a year when the timing works is also an option.

> Written from a personal visit. The impressions and opinions are my own. Festival schedules change every year, so treat this as **(as of Nov 2026)** and please check the official notice once more before you go.
