**For years I assumed this was the original location.** Turns out the real original is elsewhere in the same county, on Donghae-daero. (I haven't been to that shop yet.)

## The shop I thought was the original

Whenever I have a reason to be in Gangwon, I make a point of stopping here on the way there or back, even if it means a detour. That should tell you how many times I've been — this place was always my benchmark. It was only recently that I learned the **real original location is at 54 Donghae-daero**, also in Yangyang-gun, and this is actually the Powolwitmal-gil branch. Several unrelated restaurants use the same "Donghae Makguksu" name, which makes it easy to mix them up.

## The buckwheat and the house sauce are a perfect match

The density of the buckwheat noodles and this shop's seasoning sauce pair really well. Mix it together and take a bite — the texture of the buckwheat and the seasoning never fight each other, they just work. Before you know it, the bowl is empty.

## If there's room, get the suyuk too

If you have the appetite, I'd recommend adding suyuk. The makguksu alone is enough, but pairing it with suyuk turns the meal into something more filling and satisfying.

## The kind of place that makes you want to plan a Gangwon trip

Every so often I catch myself wondering if I should plan a Gangwon trip just because of this place. It's the kind of meal that leaves you happy after the last slurp.

## The mul-makguksu

A stainless-steel bowl is filled with a dark broth, scattered with shredded seaweed and sesame. In the middle, a neat mound of noodles carries a red seasoning paste and half a boiled egg. The broth looks almost black in its depth, and the noodles are the grey-brown of buckwheat.

## Makguksu menu words

- **Mul-makguksu** is makguksu in cold broth, and **bibim-makguksu** is makguksu mixed with seasoning sauce
- **Suyuk** is boiled pork sliced thick, and **sari** means an extra serving of noodles
- **Memil jjin-mandu** are steamed dumplings made with buckwheat dough, a common side at makguksu restaurants

## For foreign visitors

- **Makguksu is cold buckwheat noodles.** Bibim-makguksu is mixed with a sweet-spicy sauce; mul-makguksu comes in a cold broth
- This shop's spice level **isn't particularly strong**, so it's worth trying even if you're not used to spicy food
- **Suyuk is boiled pork.** Pairing it with the noodles makes for a heartier meal
