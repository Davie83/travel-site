Ganghwa Island is somewhere I drive out to now and then. Donmak Beach is the one I usually end up stopping at — it is still, by a fair margin, the best-known beach on the island.

## Changed a fair bit, but still worth it

Over the years this stretch has visibly developed — more cafes, a more finished feel than I remember. It hasn't lost its old character entirely, though, and it is still a good spot to stop for a change of scenery.

A wooden boardwalk runs through the pine trees straight down to the sand and the water. It's an easy, well-kept walk in — no real effort involved.

![A wooden boardwalk through the pine trees at Donmak Beach, with tents pitched on the sand beside it](/assets/img/incheon-ganghwa-donmak-beach-boardwalk.jpg)

## High tide, low tide, mudflat

This is a West Sea beach, so the scenery shifts completely with the tide. At high tide it's an ordinary beach. At low tide, the mudflat stretches out for a long way. Various creatures live in it, but collecting anything is banned to protect the ecosystem — look, don't take.

Large "Donmak" block letters stand on the sand. There's no mistaking where you are, and it's a solid photo spot — the mudflat and sea fill the frame behind it.

## You can pitch a tent here

One practical thing worth knowing — there's a campground in the pine woods behind the beach, and for a modest site fee you can pitch a tent and stay the night. Deck sites run around 25,000 won a night, ground sites around 20,000, with hourly rates too. Parking is 2,000 won.

So it works either as a quick day drive or as a spot to camp overnight if you bring a tent.

## Good to know

- **Donmak Beach** is on the south side of Ganghwa Island, the island's best-known stretch of sand.
- Swimming is **summer-only**. Outside that window it's mostly a walking, photo, and mudflat-watching spot.
- Whatever you see in the mudflat at low tide, **look but don't collect** — it's protected.
- The pine-woods campground lets you **camp for a site fee** — bring your own tent.
- Ganghwa is much easier by **car** than by public transit, though buses to Ganghwa run from Hapjeong and Sinchon in Seoul.

## Before you go

- **Location** — 1481 Haeannam-ro, Hwado-myeon, Ganghwa-gun, Incheon. On the south side of the island; a car makes this much easier.
- **Parking / fees** — Parking is 2,000 won. Camping runs roughly 25,000 won/night for a deck site, 20,000 for ground (hourly rates also available).
- **Season** — Swimming runs July 3 – October 31, 2026. Outside that window, only the beach and walking paths are open.
- **Pairing it up** — If you need food near the island's entrance, [the grilled-fish set at Ganghwa's seaside restaurant](/en/posts/incheon-ganghwa-saengseongui) is also on Ganghwa Island — though it's a bit of a drive from Hwado-myeon, so check the route before pairing them in one trip.

> Based on a personal visit. How the place feels can vary by person, and campground/parking fees and the swimming season can change — check current details before you go.
