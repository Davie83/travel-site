In Ganghwa-eup, Ganghwa-gun, Incheon, Ganghwa Pungmul Market is a market on Ganghwa Island where you shop on the first floor and eat on the second.

## Parking was much easier this time

Parking used to be a headache, but the car park seems to have been enlarged since. Thanks to that, I parked with far less trouble this time.

## Not big, but it has what you need

Ganghwa Pungmul Market is not very large, but it feels like a market where the things you need are concentrated in one place. Unlike a typical market, the shops sit inside a building, with the first floor given over to shops selling goods and the second floor to a food village.

## First floor — turnip kimchi and dried fish

Perhaps because of the season, most people were buying ready-made turnip kimchi, Ganghwa's speciality, or the turnips themselves. On the seafood side, dried fish seemed to be selling well.

![A seafood stall on the first floor of Ganghwa Pungmul Market, with dried fish laid out on baskets and a heap of tiny dried shrimp on the right](/assets/img/incheon-ganghwa-pungmul-market-fish.jpg)

The photo shows a seafood stall on the first floor. Flattened, dried fish are laid out neatly on round blue and green baskets, and on the right small dried shrimp-like things are heaped in a wooden box and a large bowl. Behind the stall, fluorescent lights run along the ceiling and more shops stretch into the depth of the market.

## Second floor — a market or a restaurant? It is packed

The second floor was so full of people that it was hard to tell whether it was a market or a restaurant. Most of them were eating a bandaengi set meal, something like a grilled-fish set. It is grilled bandaengi served with side dishes, like a baekban. Some shops also include marinated crab, and each shop does it differently.

![The food-floor aisle on the second floor of Ganghwa Pungmul Market, with restaurant signs on both sides and a large bandaengi set-meal sign](/assets/img/incheon-ganghwa-pungmul-market-food.jpg)

A sign at a shop on the right reads "bandaengi set meal, for 2, 35,000" **(as of Oct 2026)**. Customers sit around the tables eating.

## Leaving the market with what I needed

I bought the things I needed and walked out of a market I had not seen in a long while.

## Words to know

- **Pungmul market** is a market that gathers local products and food
- **Sunmu** is a Ganghwa speciality, a root vegetable that looks a little like a radish. When it is made into kimchi it is called **sunmu kimchi**
- **Bandaengi** is a small, flat fish from the West Sea
- **Marinated crab (kkotgejang)** is a side dish of blue crab cured in seasoning or soy sauce
- **Dried fish (geoneomul)** means dried fish and seafood

## For visitors from abroad

One building holds both the first-floor market and the second-floor restaurants, so you can shop and then go up to eat in one stop. The side dishes in a bandaengi set meal differ from shop to shop, so check on the sign or menu first whether marinated crab is included. Many shops show the price in big letters on the sign, so choosing is not hard.

## Who it suits

- ✅ You want to take home Ganghwa turnip kimchi or dried fish
- ✅ You want to see a market and eat a bandaengi set meal in one place
- ✅ You are driving and want a market with easy parking
- ⚠️ The second floor gets very crowded at mealtimes

## Before you go

- **Pairing** — if you want grilled fish instead of bandaengi, [Ganghwa Charcoal Grilled Fish](/en/posts/incheon-ganghwa-saengseongui) is also on Ganghwa Island. The market is in Ganghwa-eup, though, so check the route before you plan around it.
