The day my regular shad restaurant turned out to be closed, I walked into the shop next door instead — and ended up switching for good.

## The day my regular spot was shut

I went to **Geumsan Sikdang** nearby for years. This time it was closed, so I tried Oseong next door — and **found the dressing here had more savoury depth.** I'll be coming back to this one.

That's a preference, not a verdict. Geumsan has 1,461 Google reviews and is the street's landmark; Oseong has 66. **The famous one and the one that suited my palate turned out to be different shops.**

## Turns out the whole street serves the same thing

After switching, I learned why it didn't matter much: the **Haeyang Centre** building at Yeonan Pier holds a cluster of long-running shad restaurants, and **their main menu is essentially identical.**

- **The standard set** — raw shad salad plus soy-marinated crab
- **What differs by shop** — the dressing, and which side dishes arrive with it

**Bandaengi (밴댕이)**, a small oily fish in the herring family, is a Yeonan Pier speciality served here as *hoe-muchim*: raw, sliced, and tossed in a spicy gochujang dressing with julienned vegetables and sesame. Alongside it comes **ganjang gejang**, raw crab cured in soy sauce. Both dishes are raw — if that doesn't work for you, this whole street isn't the place.

So "which one is best" comes down to seasoning and side dishes. **You won't go badly wrong walking into any of them.**

## Eat it as a salad first, then as a rice bowl

This dish has a set order to it.

1. **Eat the shad salad on its own first.** Wrapping it in the lettuce provided works well
2. **Then tip it onto the rice and mix it in**, and eat it as a rice bowl

Don't mix everything at the start. A while as a salad, then the remainder mixed into rice to finish — one order, two different things.

![The full table — raw shad salad and soy-marinated crab with lettuce, simmered soy beans, seasoned seaweed, acorn jelly, kimchi and soybean soup](/assets/img/incheon-oseong-sikdang-table.jpg)

The table brings the raw shad salad (the main event), the soy-marinated crab (standard with it), a generous bowl of lettuce for wrapping, simmered soy beans, seasoned seaweed, acorn jelly, cabbage kimchi, and soybean paste soup. Those side dishes are exactly the part that varies shop to shop.

## Worth knowing before you go

- **It's spicy** — a gochujang dressing, so expect real heat
- **No subway.** Yeonan Pier needs a bus from Incheon Station, or a taxi. Driving is easiest
- **Closes at 9:00 PM**
- **Staying in Incheon a bit longer?** — [Paradise City Cimer](/en/posts/incheon-paradise-cimer), a spa on Yeongjong Island, is another stop worth working into the trip
- **Ordering** — a short menu at an old market-building restaurant. Pointing works

## Who this suits

- ✅ You want a regional Korean dish rather than a Seoul restaurant
- ✅ You like the idea of eating it as a salad, then as a rice bowl
- ✅ An old commercial-building restaurant with no tourist polish appeals to you
- ⚠️ Both main dishes are **raw seafood**
- ⚠️ Awkward to reach without a car
