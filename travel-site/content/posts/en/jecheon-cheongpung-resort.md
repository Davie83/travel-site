Have you heard of the Cheongpung lakeside? It's a lake village in Jecheon, in North Chungcheong province.

The scenery is genuinely good. Do nothing, just look at the water, and you find yourself unwinding.

## The Cheongpung lakeside

Cheongpung Lake was formed in 1985 when the Chungju Dam went up. Its official name is Chungju Lake, but for the stretch of water on the Cheongpung side of Jecheon, everyone says Cheongpung-ho. The water winds in between the hills, so whichever way you look, there's a mountain layered behind the lake.

The resort sits on a hill looking down over that shoreline. Step onto the balcony and the water and Bibongsan are right in front of you. On an overcast day the ridges blur into layers; on a clear day the colour of the water changes.

![Cheongpung Lake and the mountains wrapped around it — the view from Cheongpung Resort](/assets/img/jecheon-cheongpung-resort-hoban.jpg)

## The facilities are old, but

I'll be straight. The resort itself is quite old, and neither the rooms nor the shared facilities are what you'd picture from a modern resort. Fresh linens, a polished lobby — expect those and you may be let down.

Approach it by location, though, and it reads differently. This is **a place you come for the view**. That the window is basically Cheongpung Lake is almost the whole point of the stay.

## What's around

- **Cheongpung Cultural Heritage Complex** — old buildings from villages that would have gone under the water, moved and gathered here. Right next to the resort.
- **Cheongpung Lake cable car** — runs up to the summit of Bibongsan. The sharp peak in the photo is Bibongsan.
- **Sightseeing monorail · Cheongpung Land** — a monorail that loops along the lake, plus things like bungee jumping.
- **Cruise boats** run on the lake too.

## For foreign visitors

- This suits someone **who isn't in Korea for the first time**. If you've already seen the big cities like Seoul and Busan and want a different texture of the country, the inland lake regions are one answer.
- Route-wise, you can **bend a Busan/Gyeongju itinerary slightly** to slot in a day here, or pair it with Gangwon (Wonju, Yeongwol). It's about two hours by car from Seoul.
- **A car makes it much easier.** By public transport you can reach Jecheon Station or the intercity bus terminal and then take a local bus, but services are infrequent.
- The resort's facilities are worn. This fits travel that puts **the view out the window** ahead of the grade of the room.
- English signage is limited. The Cultural Heritage Complex is about the one place with explanatory panels.

## Who this suits

- ✅ You need a day of **doing nothing but looking at water**
- ✅ You're on an inland trip **driving through Jecheon, Danyang and Chungju**
- ✅ The **view** matters more to you than the room itself
- ⚠️ You're expecting a like-new resort with tidy shared facilities
- ⚠️ You're moving by public transport only — just getting here is a hassle

## Before you go

- **Getting there** — 1798 Cheongpung-ho-ro, Cheongpung-myeon, Jecheon. Right beside the Cheongpung Cultural Heritage Complex, about 30 minutes by car from central Jecheon.
- **Rooms** — book with the age of the place in mind. The thing to check is whether you're getting a lake-facing room.
- **Bundling** — Cheongpung Cultural Heritage Complex → Cheongpung Lake cable car → Danyang (Dodamsambong, Mancheonha Skywalk) makes a natural two-day loop.

> Based on a personal visit. The state of the facilities and the operation of nearby attractions can change — check before you book.
