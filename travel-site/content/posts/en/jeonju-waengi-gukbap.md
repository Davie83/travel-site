Everyone knows Jeonju is famous for kongnamul-gukbap. For me, though, this one place stands apart from the rest.

## The bowl that tells me I've arrived

Whenever I'm in Jeonju, this is the first place I stop for kongnamul-gukbap. I never set out to make it a rule — it just became one. At this point, eating here feels less like a meal and more like a notification: *you've arrived in Jeonju.*

## Old, and well-known

This place is old, and it's well-known — well-known enough that there's now a second branch alongside the original. It has built its reputation on a single dish, and the menu stays that simple.

The clean taste comes down to the broth. They simmer pesticide-free bean sprouts generously in an anchovy stock, and the result is genuinely refreshing. The sprouts stay crisp, and if you want more, you can ask for a refill.

![A stone bowl of kongnamul-gukbap on the table, with egg and kimchi side dishes nearby](/assets/img/jeonju-waengi-gukbap.jpg)

## The day I got a double-yolk egg

Instead of a poached egg, they give you a raw egg on the side. Stir it into the broth so it half-cooks, or eat it separately — whatever you prefer. The day I visited, the egg I got turned out to have a double yolk. It felt like a small sign the day was going to go well.

## Spice is up to you

I like things spicy, so I added a generous amount of cheongyang chili. The broth itself isn't spicy at all, so add chili if you want heat, or skip it if you don't — it's entirely down to preference.

## Good to know

- **Kongnamul-gukbap** is a clear soup loaded with bean sprouts, served over rice. The base version is not spicy.
- A **raw egg** comes on the side. Stir it into the hot broth to half-cook it, or eat it on its own.
- **Bean sprouts are refillable** — just ask if you'd like more.
- Want it spicy? Add **cheongyang chili** to taste.
- **No takeout** — it's dine-in only.
- There's **parking** on site, so it's easy to reach by car too.

## Before you go

- **Location** — 88 Dongmun-gil, Wansan-gu, Jeonju, near Jeonju Hanok Village.
- **Hours** — Daily 7:00 AM–9:00 PM, said to be open year-round.
- **Price** — 9,000 won for a bowl of kongnamul-gukbap.
- **Note** — There's a second branch nearby too, worth checking if the main shop is packed.
- **Elsewhere in Jeonbuk** — in Buan, I've also been to [Halmae Pisundae](/en/posts/buan-halmae-pisundae). It's a fair drive from Jeonju though, so plan it as a separate outing rather than pairing them same-day.
