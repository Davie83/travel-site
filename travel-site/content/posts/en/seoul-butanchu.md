**I moved away, so I rarely get there now — but it used to be an occasional stop.**

## A place where you choose

This is the character of the shop. **You pick the noodle type, and you pick how rich the broth is.**

In the menu names, **"toko" means the rich version.** So it splits like this.

| Name | Character |
|---|---|
| **Toko** tonkotsu | **The rich one** |
| Shio tonkotsu | The clean one (salt) |
| Shoyu tonkotsu | Soy sauce |

On top of that you can specify the noodles, the sauce, and how much garlic, spring onion and bean sprouts you want. **A heavy Japanese-style tonkotsu** is the closest description.

## How I order it

**When I go, I have the toko tonkotsu with chijiremen.**

Chijiremen are curly noodles. With a broth on the heavy side, I find noodles that hold the soup suit it better. There is no right answer — plenty of people prefer thin straight noodles.

**The gyoza set turns it into a full meal.** If one bowl of ramen is not enough, go that way.

## Honestly — it is not spectacular

**It is not spectacular. But something about it keeps pulling me back.**

Both sentences have to sit together to be accurate. This is not a place you seek out to be amazed; it is **a place that comes to mind now and then.** I think those are worth recording too.

Set your expectation to **"a bowl of tonkotsu you get to configure"** rather than "the best ramen in Hongdae", and it lands well.

## Location

**Three minutes on foot from exit 7 of Hongik Univ. Station.** It sits in the middle of Hongdae, so it is easy to slot into another plan.

There are nights in Hongdae when a bowl of ramen is exactly how you want to finish. Bear in mind that **the door closes at 10 PM**, so get in before then.

## For international visitors

**Tonkotsu ramen is Japanese ramen in a heavy broth simmered from pork bones.** Not Korean food, but an easy meal in Hongdae, so it is worth noting.

- **Not spicy.** A little chili powder may be sprinkled on top, as in the photo, but it is not a spicy dish
- **The broth is pork.** Not suitable if you avoid pork — the chashu on top is pork too
- **They will ask you several questions** when you order: noodle type, broth richness, amounts of garlic, spring onion and bean sprouts. If unsure, just say **"standard, please"**
- **The broth can be very rich and salty.** For something cleaner, order shio tonkotsu rather than the *toko* version
- **The noodles are wheat.** Not suitable if you avoid gluten
- **You may lift the bowl and drink the broth.** That is not rude in a Japanese or Korean ramen shop
- **Gyoza are pan-fried dumplings.** The set portion is generous

## Go if

- ✅ You want to **choose your noodles and broth strength**
- ✅ You like heavy tonkotsu
- ✅ You want **a filling meal** during a Hongdae day
- ✅ **You cannot eat spicy food** — this place is safe

- ⚠️ **It is a pork-bone broth.** No good if you avoid pork
