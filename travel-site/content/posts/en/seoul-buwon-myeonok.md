A cold noodle house on the **second floor** inside Namdaemun Market. If you came to Korea wanting the old, restrained style of *Pyongyang naengmyeon*, this is a satisfying place to find it.

## What Pyongyang naengmyeon actually is — read this first

This is the dish most likely to disappoint a first-time visitor, so it's worth being honest.

*Naengmyeon* here means **buckwheat noodles in a cold, clear beef broth.** There is no chilli, no sugar hit, no strong seasoning. Many Koreans describe their first bowl as **"tasting like nothing."**

That blankness is the point. You are meant to taste the buckwheat and the broth itself, not a sauce. Think closer to a delicate Japanese *dashi* than to anything spicy — this is not the fiery Korean food most travellers expect.

**This restaurant keeps to the old, restrained version** rather than the richer style currently fashionable in Seoul. That's exactly why people who love the dish come here.

- **If you already like Pyongyang naengmyeon** — you'll be happy
- **If it's your first time** — come knowing this is the traditional, subtle end of the spectrum

**The mustard and vinegar on the table are for you to add.** That is the normal way to eat it: add a little at a time until the bowl tastes right to you. Nobody will think you're doing it wrong.

Also note: **you will not find the red, spicy version here.** If you want spicy cold noodles, that's a different dish (*bibim-naengmyeon*) at a different kind of restaurant.

## Order the bindaeddeok — it's fried in beef fat

**The mung bean pancake is pan-fried in meat fat**, which makes it deeply nutty and savoury in a way the vegetable-oil version never is.

It's small. That's a feature — it's **light enough to add without filling you up.** And it works: the noodles are deliberately plain, the pancake is rich. Order them together and treat it as one meal rather than two dishes.

## What the neighbouring tables order

Sit for a while and you'll see nearby tables occasionally ordering **spicy dressed pork** (*jeyuk-muchim*) and **spicy dressed chicken** (*dak-muchim*). Those are the other things that come out of this kitchen.

Worth knowing if you're a group and want something with more punch alongside the noodles.

## The value is the other reason to come

Pyongyang naengmyeon has become an expensive dish in Seoul. **Here you get the old flavour at market prices** — the taste-to-price ratio is about as good as it gets.

## Finding it — this is the tricky part

The address is **41-6 Namdaemunsijang 4-gil, and it is on the 2nd floor.** It sits in a market lane, and you have to go upstairs. Scanning ground-floor shopfronts, you will walk straight past it. Use the map link.

Come in through Namdaemun Market from **Hoehyeon Station (Line 4).**

**It closes at 8:00 PM** — earlier than most Seoul restaurants, so don't save it for a late dinner. Opening time and closed days need checking.

## Practical notes

- **Ordering** — a small traditional menu, so pointing works.
- **Payment** — card is standard in Seoul, but keep some cash for the market itself.
- **Combine it with** Namdaemun Market, which is already on most itineraries. This makes a good lunch stop mid-shopping.
