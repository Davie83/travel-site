In Yeongdeungpo, Seoul, Gangnam is a Chinese-Korean restaurant on the second floor of an apartment-complex arcade right in front of Dangsan Station, and it is the place I go to for a bowl of seafood-packed samseon jjamppong.

## It is called Gangnam, but it is in Dangsan

The restaurant is named Gangnam. It is not in Gangnam, though. It sits on the second floor of the arcade building of an apartment complex, directly in front of Dangsan Station. If you head to the Gangnam district because of the name you will have made a wasted trip, so get the location right first.

## A place only people in the know come to

You could call it a place only locals in the know come to. For a start, the portions are generous across the menu. Even a single bowl gives you a sense of plenty before you take the first bite.

## Why I come here: the samseon jjamppong

What brings me here is the samseon jjamppong. I have three reasons.

- **Springy noodles** — the noodles are chewy
- **A balanced broth** — the seafood stock does not lean too far in any direction
- **A lot of seafood** — it is packed with it, and there is plenty of nakji (small octopus) too

Even though I am a man and I go when I am hungry, I have never once finished a bowl. That is how generous it is.

## This time: samseon jjamppong and tangsuyuk

This day I had the samseon jjamppong with tangsuyuk. The tangsuyuk is old-style, and its sauce leans noticeably toward ketchup. I rarely eat tangsuyuk sauce, so I either eat the pork plain or dip it in a sauce of soy sauce mixed with chili flakes (gochugaru). That suits me better.

## What it looks like

On the left is a large plate with a red scroll-and-flower pattern around the rim, piled with pieces of fried pork, and a small bowl of red sauce with carrot and onion sits at the top of the plate. On the right is a big bowl with a red rim holding an orange broth, topped with cabbage, carrot, what look like bamboo shoots, mushrooms, squid, shrimp and nakji (small octopus). A dish of white pickled radish sits at the lower right, and a small black dish of dark sauce sits at the bottom center.

## If you like fried dumplings, try the yakki mandu

Other dishes here are good too. If you like fried dumplings, I recommend the yakki mandu on the menu. The wrappers are thin and crisp, and perhaps because they are fried at high heat, the filling is full of juice.

## Menu notes

- **Samseon jjamppong (삼선짬뽕)** is a red, spicy Chinese-Korean noodle soup with plenty of seafood. "Samseon" is the word Korean Chinese restaurants put in front of dishes loaded with seafood
- **Tangsuyuk (탕수육)** is fried pork served with a sweet-and-sour sauce. This place makes the old-style version
- **Yakki mandu (야끼만두)** is the name for fried dumplings, either deep-fried or pan-fried
- To order, say "Samseon-jjamppong juseyo" (삼선짬뽕 주세요)

## For visitors

- **How to find it** — it is on the second floor of the apartment arcade right in front of Dangsan Station (Lines 2 and 9). You go up to the second floor, not the first
- **If you do not like spicy food** — jjamppong is a red, spicy noodle soup. If you would prefer a soup-free black-bean noodle, the menu also has samseon jjajang and gan-jjajang (as of Oct 2026)
- **If you are going in a group** — tangsuyuk comes in a large size, and the tray jjajang is ordered in portions for two (as of Oct 2026)

## Who it suits

- ✅ People who want a jjamppong packed with seafood
- ✅ People who like chewy noodles
- ✅ People looking for a Chinese-Korean restaurant with generous portions
- ✅ People who love crisp, juicy fried dumplings
- ⚠️ The tangsuyuk sauce is old-style with a strong ketchup note, which not everyone will like

## Before you go

- **Closed days and hours** — it is closed on Mondays, and the break time is 3:00~4:30 PM, so avoid the gap between lunch and dinner (as of Oct 2026)
- **Nearby** — [Pho Ong Nam Dangsan](/posts/seoul-pho-ong-nam), also near Dangsan Station, is easy to follow with a walk in Han River Park. If you feel like dim sum in Yeongdeungpo, there is also [Hao'sum Yeongdeungpo](/posts/seoul-haosum-yeongdeungpo).
