Yeouido is Seoul's finance district — Korea's Manhattan, and priced like it. **Finding good value at lunch here is the hard part**, and this place is the answer.

## What *hoedeopbap* actually is

The main dish here is **hoedeopbap (회덮밥)** — and you should know what you're ordering.

It is **raw fish over rice**, served with shredded vegetables, flying fish roe, dried seaweed and a squeeze of *gochujang* (fermented chilli paste). Think of it as a Korean cousin of *chirashi* or poke, but built to be **mixed, not eaten in layers.**

**The fish is raw and not cured.** If you don't eat raw fish, order something else — this is a seafood restaurant with other options.

## The side dishes are the story

Korean restaurants serve free side dishes (*banchan*), refillable at no charge. Here they go further than usual.

**One of the side dishes is a breaded fried fish cutlet** — with tartar sauce and a small salad. In most places that is a paid main course. Here it arrives as one plate among several.

The full spread in the photo:

- **Kkakdugi** (cubed radish kimchi) and **cabbage kimchi** — two kinds
- **Simmered soy beans** with sesame
- **Braised radish** in a mildly spicy sauce
- **The fried fish cutlet** with tartar sauce and salad

You could make a meal of the sides alone. **The number of plates relative to the lunch price** is exactly what makes this good value.

## How to eat the bowl

**Mix it.** Turn the ingredients over from the bottom so the chilli paste coats the rice throughout — that is how the dish is designed to work.

Practical tip: **mix it halfway, taste, then add more chilli paste** if you want it stronger. Once it's all in, you can't take it back.

And there is one more thing. **Order just the rice bowl and a spicy fish stew still arrives alongside it, in its own earthenware pot.** It is not a token bowl of broth — it comes properly loaded, and **it tastes good.**

This matters more than it sounds. Hoedeopbap is a cold dish; a hot stew next to it changes the balance of the whole meal. Counting that in, the amount of food for the lunch price stands out even more.

The bowl comes with **a lot of shredded cabbage**, which matters — hoedeopbap can leave you hungry by mid-afternoon, and the vegetable volume here prevents that.

## Private rooms — useful to know

Beyond the main hall, there are **separate private rooms.**

- Quiet meals with a small group
- **Hosting guests** properly

Yeouido runs on business meals, and this restaurant is set up for them. The lunch course is called **teuk-hoe-jeongsik (특회정식)**, and it works for occasions where you need to look after someone properly. It is a popular order here.

One reviewer listed the sequence in full:

> aged sashimi → clam soup → corn cheese → poached octopus → live octopus → sea cucumber and sea squirt → seasoned squid → soy-marinated crab → grilled mackerel → fried spring rolls → spicy fish stew (full of roe)

Eleven courses, served in order. That is more than enough for hosting someone.
