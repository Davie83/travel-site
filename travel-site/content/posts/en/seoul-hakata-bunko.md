This is **one of the oldest Japanese ramen shops in Seoul**. I have gone back now and then over the years — and looking back, it was **always late at night**.

## Open until 3 AM

That is this shop's real strength.

**It opens at 11:30 AM and closes at 3:00 AM.** When you have been out in Hongdae and you need an actual meal rather than a snack, a ramen shop still open at that hour is rare.

I have driven over more than once on a sudden late-night craving. **The fact that they stay open that late is something to be grateful for.**

## Inramen with a heap of garlic

I order it the same way every time. **Inramen, with plenty of minced garlic.**

That is the garlic you can see on the right of the photo. **Peeled cloves and a press sit on the table, and you crush them in yourself.** This is standard practice at a Hakata ramen shop.

Once the garlic steeps into the thick tonkotsu broth, the bowl becomes a different thing. **It works as hangover food and as a restorative at the same time.** At 2 in the morning, that combination lands.

## The noodles are thin

**Hakata style means thin noodles.** In the photo you can see them in the broth, nearly thread-thin.

I prefer thin noodles. The broth clings to them better, and they go down differently.

The trade-off is that **thin noodles go soft fast.** Eat them as soon as the bowl arrives. That is exactly why Hakata shops in Japan let you choose noodle firmness.

## Being honest about it

**Hongdae is a ramen battleground.** There are several shops nearby rated 4.5 to 4.6. Hakata Bunko sits at **4.1**.

The reviews explain why. Some people find it salty, and one review notes that the line used to be long but there are simply many more ramen shops now.

Those same reviews also say this:

> **"Say what you like, for me this is the standard for Japanese ramen."**
> **"A flavour that does the basics properly."**

This shop has **held the same spot for over 20 years**. So the fair way to put it is this:

- **Looking for the best bowl of ramen in the area?** Hongdae has higher-rated options
- **Looking for benchmark Hakata ramen, and a place that is actually open at 2 AM?** This is it

## Good to know

- **The broth is heavy.** Tonkotsu is pork bone simmered for hours into an opaque white soup. If you want something light, this is not it
- **It is not spicy.** The standard inramen has no chilli in it
- **Extra chashu** is the popular add-on, and reviewers mention it constantly
- **Cheongramen** is a separate item on the menu. Ask in the shop what makes it different

## Who will like this

- ✅ You like Hakata-style tonkotsu and thin noodles
- ✅ You enjoy adding your own garlic at the table
- ✅ You need a real meal late at night or in the early hours
- ✅ You cannot handle spicy food
- ⚠️ The broth is rich and some find it salty
- ⚠️ Thin noodles soften quickly, so start eating right away
