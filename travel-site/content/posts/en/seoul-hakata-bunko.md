I first went about 15 years ago, and I went again this year. Hakata Bunko is one of Seoul's older Japanese ramen shops, a place I drop in on now and then when I get the craving.

## 🌙 The ramen shop I think of at night

Looking back, I have **always gone late**. When I had been out late in Hongdae and needed a proper meal, I have rushed over more than once after suddenly thinking of it. It opens at 11:30 AM and closes at 3 AM (as of Aug 2026), and a ramen shop near Hongdae that stays open this late is rare. My recent visits weren't at that hour, though, so I haven't confirmed that it is still open until then. If you are going late, it is safer to check ahead.

## ⏳ This year I waited for just one party

The lines used to be long, but there are many more shops around Hongdae now. On my last visit this year I waited for one party and went straight in.

## 🧄 Inramen with plenty of garlic

My way of eating is always the same: **I crush plenty of garlic into the Inramen.** Peeled garlic and a press sit on the table so you can crush it in yourself, which is the standard at a Hakata ramen shop. Once the garlic flavour opens up in the thick tonkotsu broth, the taste goes up a notch. It feels like a hangover cure and a tonic at once.

Tonkotsu is a milky broth made by simmering pork bones for a long time, and chashu is a slice of pork braised in soy seasoning. In the photo, a white bowl with a red scroll pattern around the rim holds milky tonkotsu broth, with thin noodles lying beneath it. A heap of finely cut scallion covers the top, along with bean sprouts, half a soy-braised egg, one thick slice of chashu and thin-sliced wood-ear mushroom. A white lace-patterned spoon rests on one side; behind the bowl are a dish of crushed garlic and a garlic press, and a thin film of oil floats on the broth.

## 🍜 The noodles are thin

**Hakata-style noodles are thin.** In the photo too, the noodles in the broth are as thin as thread. I prefer thin noodles, because the broth clings to them and they slide down differently. The weakness is that they go soft quickly, so it is better to eat as soon as it arrives.

Besides the Inramen I have tried the **Cheongramen**, and I have also had a spicy item that can only be ordered at certain times of day. I can't remember its name.

## 🧂 Being honest

**Hongdae is a ramen battleground.** There are several places nearby rated 4.5–4.6, while Hakata Bunko is 4.1. My one complaint is that the broth can taste salty. Still, for **sticking to the basics**, it is like a standard of Japanese-style ramen, and it has held the same spot for over 20 years. The answer depends on what you want.

- **If you want the very best ramen** — there are higher-rated places in Hongdae
- **If you want a benchmark Hakata ramen, and somewhere open late** — this is it

## 🌐 For foreign visitors

- The broth is rich. Tonkotsu is a cloudy broth from long-simmered pork bones, so it isn't right if you want something light
- It isn't spicy. The basic Inramen has no chilli seasoning
- Extra chashu is a popular combination
- The garlic press is on the table. Press in as much as you like

## 👥 When to go

- ✅ You like Hakata-style tonkotsu and thin noodles, and enjoy adding garlic yourself
- ✅ You need a proper meal late at night
- ✅ You can't eat spicy food
- ✅ If you also want somewhere to rest in Hongdae around your ramen — [Roofcatme Cat Cafe, Hongdae](/en/posts/seoul-roofcatme-hongdae), in front of Hongik Univ. Station Exit 9, is within walking distance
- ⚠️ The broth is rich and can taste salty
- ⚠️ Thin noodles soften fast — eat as soon as it arrives
