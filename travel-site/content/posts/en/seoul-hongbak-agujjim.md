It's a bit stuffy being in a basement, but this is **the place I think of when I think of boiled monkfish.** Even as I write this, I want to eat it.

## 🪜 It's in a basement, and nearly full on weekday evenings

It isn't far from Gongdeok Station, but the restaurant is in a basement, so it is easy to walk past if you only look at signs from the street. You have to go down the stairs. Being underground does feel a bit stuffy, but there are no grilled dishes, so ventilation isn't really a problem. I went on a weekday evening, and although by chance I have never had to wait, the place was nearly full each time. Gongdeok Station is served by Line 5, Line 6, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line and the AREX airport line, so you can come from Incheon Airport in one go.

## 🍲 I go for the suyuk

They sell both the boiled suyuk and the spicy jjim. Every time I decide, "this time I'll have the spicy jjim," but once I arrive I can't resist the suyuk, so it ends up being suyuk every time. I still haven't tried the jjim. Another reason I go for the suyuk is that the jjim doesn't come with the monkfish liver. The suyuk is made from fresh monkfish boiled really well, and the flesh is tender.

## 🧈 The liver is creamy, like foie gras

The real draw here is the **monkfish liver.** It is soft like cream and tastes a bit like foie gras, and its flavour is outstanding. Honestly, I sometimes think I go because I crave the liver.

![The table at Hongbak Agujjim — the boiled monkfish platter with radish salad, pickled chilli, dried anchovies, egg pancake and sikhye](/assets/img/seoul-hongbak-agujjim-table.jpg)

In the photo, a large white plate with a wavy rim holds heaped monkfish flesh and bone pieces, with bean sprouts, green onion and radish mixed in. The cream-coloured lump in the middle, different from the flesh, is the liver. The broth isn't red with chilli flakes but a pale yellow, a clear steamed look, and around it are stir-fried anchovies, kimchi, a red seasoned side, stir-fried vegetables and a small dish of gochujang. The small side dishes are good too.

## 👥 Small for two or three, large for four

Even the small size feeds two or three. If someone eats a lot, three people will fall short, and I think the large would be plenty for four. Splitting the 60,000 KRW small between two comes to about 30,000 per person, so it isn't a cheap place. If you are alone, there is monkfish soup at lunch.

## 🌐 For visitors

- Agwi is monkfish, a sea fish with a large mouth and chewy flesh. In Korea it is often eaten as jjim (a dish steamed or braised with seasoning) or in soup
- It is not a smelly dish. The broth is clear and light, with almost no seasoning
- The suyuk isn't spicy. The spicy one is the jjim, so if you can't eat spicy food, order the suyuk
- The liver is rich. It is creamy and nutty with a strong sea scent, so it suits you if you like Japanese ankimo or Western foie gras
- The skin is chewy and slippery. It is the translucent grey pieces in the photo, and the texture may be unfamiliar

## 📅 Before you go

It is closed on Sundays, and the break from 3 to 5 PM means a late lunch can end in a wasted trip. If you want a cafe after the meal, [Fritz Coffee Dohwa](/en/posts/seoul-fritz-dohwa) in the same Mapo area is close. It suits people who like rich liver dishes such as monkfish liver, ankimo and foie gras, and anyone looking for a light fish dish eaten for the ingredient's own taste without seasoning; the slippery skin texture may be unfamiliar.
