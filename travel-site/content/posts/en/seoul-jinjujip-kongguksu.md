This is one of the shops Koreans name when they list **the best kongguksu in Seoul.** In summer the Yeouido office crowd queues for it.

## What kongguksu is

**Cold noodles in a broth of ground boiled soybeans.** A summer dish.

Look at the photo: the broth is thick and opaque. It looks like soy milk, and the important part is that **it contains no sugar and no salt.**

## You season it yourself

**The broth arrives completely unseasoned.** If you taste it as it comes and decide it is bland, you have missed the dish.

How people eat it **varies from person to person.** You add **salt or sugar** to your own taste.

- **Salt** — the more common choice in Korea, from what I see. It sharpens the nutty soybean flavour
- **Sugar** — others prefer this, depending on region and habit. It makes the broth taste rounder and softer

There is no correct answer. **Add a little, stir, taste, repeat.** That process is how the dish is eaten.

Once the seasoning lands, the broth and the noodles start working together. Find that point and this becomes something **you crave on every hot day.**

## Notes for visitors from abroad

**This will probably be unfamiliar.** It is not difficult, though.

- **It is cold.** If you order expecting a hot noodle soup you will be surprised
- **It is not spicy.** There is no chilli in the broth. The only heat is the kimchi served alongside
- **Season it yourself.** As above. Skip this step and you will not taste what the dish is
- **The noodles and broth are plant-based.** The kimchi served with it may contain fermented seafood, so if you are vegetarian it is safer to leave the kimchi

## Being honest about it

This is the part I most want to say.

**One bowl is 15,000 won.** That is expensive for a bowl of noodles. And it is the result of **years of steady increases.**

At the same time, **a lot of similar kongguksu shops have opened recently.** Even within Yeouido, more people now name somewhere else first.

So I would put it this way. **If you are willing to give up a little,** you now have options — and that makes going to Jinjujip a slightly harder decision than it used to be. The food has not got worse. **The price and the alternatives both changed.**

- **If this is your first kongguksu** — this is a good place to set your baseline. Start here
- **If you already know the dish** — whether it is worth 15,000 won is a judgement people will split on

## Before you go

- **It is in basement level 1** of a building called the Yeouido Department Store. Despite the name it is an ageing commercial block, not a modern mall. Go downstairs
- **Closed Sundays.** Saturday ends at 7 PM
- **There is a queue.** Lunchtime certainly, and reports of a ten-minute wait even around 3 PM
- **It is a summer dish.** Many shops run kongguksu seasonally, so check first if you are visiting in winter

## Who will like this

- ✅ You like cold, nutty, savoury broths
- ✅ You cannot handle spicy food
- ✅ You want to eat one proper Korean summer dish
- ✅ You enjoy seasoning a dish to your own taste at the table

- ⚠️ The broth is unseasoned. Eaten plain it tastes of very little
