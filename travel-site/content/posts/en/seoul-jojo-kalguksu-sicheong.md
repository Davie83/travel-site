When people think of the clams in kalguksu, they usually picture bajirak (short-necked clams) or a perilla-seed broth, but this place uses dongjuk instead. After a long gap, I dropped in at Jojo Kalguksu's City Hall branch.

## 🐚 Not bajirak, not perilla seed: dongjuk kalguksu

What I eat at Jojo Kalguksu is not the usual bajirak kalguksu or perilla-seed kalguksu but the **dongjuk kalguksu**. Dongjuk is a kind of surf clam that is hard to find around Seoul, so I come for the noodle soup made with it. Kalguksu is a noodle soup made by cutting dough with a knife and boiling it in broth. As far as I know the main branch is somewhere else, and this time I went to the City Hall branch.

## 🌶️ A cool-tasting, peppery broth

The other reason I come is the cool, peppery flavour I like. The broth leans toward refreshing and peppery at once, so someone who can't eat spicy food may find it hot.

![Dongjuk kalguksu at Jojo Kalguksu City Hall — a white bowl with dongjuk clams, vegetables and a crown-daisy-like sprig, with a sauce tray, kimchi and green chillies beside it](/assets/img/seoul-jojo-kalguksu-sicheong.jpg)

In the photo a white bowl is full of clear broth, and clams still in their shells show in the soup. Among the wide noodles are green vegetables and leaves that look like bok choy, and a handful of what looks like crown daisy sits in the middle. Behind the bowl, a tray divided into three holds dark brown, red and pale amber-brown sauces, and a small dish of kimchi and two green chillies are served on the side.

## ⏰ Outside meal hours, no wait

It wasn't a meal-hour rush that day, so I ate without waiting. I hadn't come because of something nearby; the craving simply hit me, so not having to wait was a plus.

## 🌐 For visitors

- Quite a few foreign visitors were there too
- To order, say "Dongjuk-kalguksu juseyo" (동죽칼국수 주세요), meaning "one dongjuk kalguksu, please"
- The broth is peppery, so if you can't eat spicy food, ask the staff when you order whether the heat can be adjusted
- The clams come in their shells. You don't eat the shells; just pick out the meat
- It is walkable from City Hall Station (Lines 1 and 2), and the road goes uphill
- The menu also has spicy octopus and seafood pajeon and mulchong clam soup, but I only had the kalguksu that day

## 🔁 This kalguksu is enough

I'd like to try other dishes, but for me the dongjuk kalguksu is enough here. It's what I came for. Thinking about the cold weather ahead, I expect to come back at least once more before this winter is over.

It suits people who like a cool, peppery broth and want to try dongjuk kalguksu, which is uncommon in Seoul. If you want a mild broth with dumplings instead, there is [Myeongdong Gyoja](/en/posts/seoul-myeongdong-gyoja), and if you walk toward Namdaemun Market, [Maknae Hoejip](/en/posts/seoul-maknae-hoejip) serves a sashimi set only at weekday lunch.
