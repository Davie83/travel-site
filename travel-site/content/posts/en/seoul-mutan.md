If you have always thought of jjajangmyeon as the cheap item at a Chinese restaurant, Mutan sits at the opposite end. It is a Chinese restaurant on the 2nd floor of TP Tower in Yeouido.

## 📅 Book ahead, and come straight from Yeouido Station

I went on a weekday evening, and the person I went with made the reservation. The place fills up even on weekday evenings, so treat a reservation as a must. It is on the 2nd floor of TP Tower and connects directly to Yeouido Station, so you can arrive without getting wet on a rainy day. Yeouido is an office district with little to see around it, but for accessibility alone it ranks among the best Chinese restaurants in Seoul.

## 🍜 A bowl like a premium jjajangmyeon

Jajangmyeon is a wheat noodle topped with a fermented black bean sauce, and one of the most popular foods in Korea. It began as a Chinese-Korean dish in Incheon but has settled fully as a Korean food, and it is sweeter, blacker and thicker than the zhajiangmian of China or the jajangmyeon at Chinese restaurants in other countries. Every neighbourhood has a cheap version, and this restaurant takes it in the direction of doing it properly.

Of the two signatures, the steak truffle jjajangmyeon was the more impressive. It felt like a premium jjajangmyeon, and it tasted good. Thinly sliced black truffle is laid over the black sauce with thick pieces of steak, and the truffle isn't skimped on, so the aroma clearly comes through. The portion is large; I couldn't finish it alone, and it is good for sharing between two.

In the photo, dark brown sauce covers the noodles thickly in a white bowl, with several layers of sliced beef on top. On one side, black truffle shaved thin sits on a soft-fried egg, and the noodles are thick and yellowish. On the plate at the back, finely cut green and red chilli are piled in a mound with sesame, over a bed of fried pieces. At the top left are a small dish of braised peanuts and a pair of tongs.

## 🌶️ The chilli on the yurinki is not spicy

Yurinki is a Chinese-style dish of fried chicken with a tangy sauce, and it is another signature here. Finely chopped green and red chillies are piled like a mountain over the crisp fried chicken, an amount that looks alarming in the photo.

> **These are the mild kind of chilli. This is not a spicy dish.** Korea uses both mild chillies and the genuinely fiery *cheongyang* type, and this is the former. The chilli is here for aroma and crunch, to cut the fat of the frying — not for heat. **If you avoid spicy food, you can still order this.**

## 🍽️ The downside: the portions are awkward

Each dish is bigger than a single serving, which makes ordering several awkward. Even two dishes for two people seems like more than you could finish, yet ordering just one for two feels odd. With about four people you can taste a bit of everything. The set menus offer decent value and beat ordering single dishes one by one.

## 💸 It's expensive

Honestly, each dish costs more than the average at a Chinese restaurant. The portion size makes up for much of it, and if you count one bowl shared by two, the cost per person comes out lower than you'd think. It runs 20,000–50,000 KRW per person: sticking to jjajangmyeon or jjamppong lands at the low end, and adding the signature dishes pushes it toward the top.

## 🌐 For visitors

- Truffle is a strongly scented, high-end mushroom, sliced thin and laid on top
- Beyond the noodles and chicken, look at **menbosha** — deep-fried shrimp toast — plus **chadol jjamppong** (spicy seafood-and-beef noodle soup, with an unusually thick broth) and **mapo tofu served in an earthenware pot**
- Cards are standard, and the menu has photos, so ordering isn't hard
- The menu centres on meat and seafood, so there aren't many choices for vegetarians

It suits people curious about a jjajangmyeon topped with truffle and steak, people who can go in a group and share two or three mains, and people who want somewhere connected directly to the subway. Without a reservation you won't get a seat in the evening, and it costs more than the average Chinese restaurant, so check the set menus. If you want a soup lunch, the pho at [Beyond Vietnam](/en/posts/seoul-beyond-vietnam) near National Assembly Station in the same Yeouido area is also worth considering.
