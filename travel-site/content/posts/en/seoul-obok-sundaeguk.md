**The boiled pork here is more tender than anywhere else I have had it.** I went in expecting the side dish a soup shop throws in. I left thinking about the pork.

And it **arrives fast.** There is no long wait after ordering, and what lands on the table that quickly is genuinely good.

## What sundaeguk is

A pork soup built on bones and offal, served with **sundae** — Korean blood sausage. It is one of the most ordinary things Koreans eat and one of the least translated.

The thing to understand from the photo: **the broth is pale and clear, not red.** Spring onion on top, black pepper, nothing else. It is not a spicy dish by default.

**You add the heat yourself.** Stir in the red seasoning paste sitting beside the bowl and it becomes spicy. Leave it alone and it stays mild.

## Order the set first

This is the practical point.

**The standalone pork plate is expensive enough to hesitate over.** So on a first visit, order the **jeongsik** — the set — which includes some of the pork alongside the soup. That much is enough to understand why people talk about the pork here.

Taste it, then come back and order it properly. Doing it the other way round just costs more.

With a group, get the **modeum** platter — the big plate in the photo, with pork, sausage and soup together.

## What is actually on the plate

Look closely and it is not one cut.

- **Lean sliced pork** — soft, mild
- **Skin** — cooked until translucent and gelatinous, chewy in a good way
- **Offal cuts** — including pig stomach, firmer and more textured

The texture keeps changing across one plate. That is what makes it worth ordering.

## Mullae or Deungchon

Obok Sundaeguk has **several branches.** I have eaten at both Mullae and Deungchon.

**Personally, Mullae — the main branch — was better.** Same name, slightly different broth and slightly different cooking. If you are travelling to it deliberately, go to Mullae.

## Before you go

- **Closed Sundays.** Confirmed on the Google Maps hours table
- **Closed 3 to 5 PM on weekdays.** A late lunch will find the door shut
- **Saturday closes at 2 PM.** No dinner service at all. This one catches people out

## Who will like this

- ✅ You like slow-boiled, tender pork
- ✅ You want to eat a genuinely everyday Korean soup, not a tourist version
- ✅ You cannot handle spicy food (skip the red paste and it is mild)
- ✅ You are already visiting Mullae Art Village, the old ironworks district next door
- ⚠️ There is offal in it. Both the texture and the smell may be unfamiliar
- ⚠️ The standalone pork plate is not cheap — start with the set
- ⚠️ Closed Sundays · closed 3-5 PM weekdays · Saturday ends at 2 PM
